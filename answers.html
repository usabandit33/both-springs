Questions people search nationally

Should both springs be replaced when one breaks?

On a standard two-spring torsion shaft they age together. Replacing one often leaves you paying another trip when the twin fails. Ask what is actually on your door before you agree.

Does the trip fee credit toward the job?

Sometimes. Sometimes not. Get the rule before the truck leaves the shop. Do not invent a number from this page.

Torsion or extension?

Torsion springs sit on a shaft above the door. Extension springs run along the horizontal tracks. The “replace the pair” question is mostly a torsion-pair question. Extension systems have their own safety-cable and stretch issues.

What does “high cycle” mean?

A cycle is one open and close. Higher-cycle springs are sold as lasting more openings. Ask for the rating they will install and whether both sides match.

Why do quotes swing so far?

Door weight, wire size, custom wind, after-hours dispatch, and whether the shop is a local yard or a national lead buyer all change the ticket. Compare written scopes, not a single phone number.

How do I spot a lead network?

Ask the person who answers where the truck is based tonight and whose name will be on the invoice. If they will not say, keep calling.

Is a broken spring a DIY job?

Torsion springs store a lot of energy. This site does not teach winding. If you hire, ask the checklist. If you do not hire, that is your call.

What should be on the invoice?

Both springs or one; cycle rating; whether the trip fee was credited; labor vs parts; warranty length in days; who to call if the door binds tomorrow.
