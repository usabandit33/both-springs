Common garage door spring questions

Clear answers for the decisions that show up right after a spring snaps — before you book a truck.

Should both springs be replaced when one breaks?

On a standard two-spring torsion shaft they age together. Replacing only the broken one often means another trip when the twin fails. Confirm what is actually on your door, then ask whether both springs will be replaced on this visit.

Does the trip fee credit toward the job?

Some shops credit the trip or service-call fee if you approve the repair the same visit. Others keep it. Ask for the rule before anyone is dispatched.

Torsion or extension?

Torsion springs sit on a shaft above the door. Extension springs run along the horizontal tracks. The “replace the pair” question is mainly a torsion-pair question. Extension systems have their own stretch and safety-cable issues.

What does “high cycle” mean?

A cycle is one open and one close. Higher-cycle springs are sold to last for more openings. Ask for the rating they will install and whether both sides match.

Why do quotes swing so far?

Door weight, wire size, custom wind, after-hours dispatch, and whether you are talking to a local shop or a national lead buyer all change the ticket. Compare written scopes, not a single phone number.

How do I spot a lead network?

Ask where the truck is based tonight and whose name will be on the invoice. If they will not say, call another number.

Is a broken spring something to fix yourself?

Torsion springs store a large amount of energy. Many people hire a technician. If you do, run the checklist: both springs, fee credit, cycle rating, arrival window, and what will appear on the invoice.

What should be on the invoice?

One spring or both; cycle rating; whether the trip fee was credited; labor vs parts; warranty length in days; who to call if the door binds tomorrow.
